Mazzamorra Morada

Mazamorra morada, which translates directly into English as "Peruvian purple corn pudding," is one of Peru's most beloved and comforting desserts, traditionally enjoyed warm during the winter and the religious celebrations of October. It is a thick, jelly-like pudding made by boiling dried Peruvian purple corn with aromatic spices like cinnamon and cloves, alongside dynamic fruits like pineapple, quince, and dried prunes. Its unique deep-purple color, fruity aroma, and distinct sweet-and-tart flavor make it a quintessential staple of limeño street food, often served side-by-side in the same cup with rice pudding (arroz con leche) to create a famous combination known as a "combinado."

Ingredients

Sub-category

  • Purple corn
  • Pineapple, dried prunes or dried peaches
  • Cinnamon sticks and cloves
  • Sugar
  • Sweet potato starch or chuño (potato starch)

Instructions

Sub-category

  1. Boil and strain: Cook the purple corn in a large pot with water, pineapple peels, cinnamon, and cloves for 45 minutes. Strain the dark liquid and discard the solids.
  2. Cook the fruit: Return the liquid to the pot. Add sugar to taste, plus small pieces of pineapple, apple, and prunes. Simmer until the fruit is soft.
  3. Thicken: Dissolve the sweet potato starch in a little cold water. Pour it slowly into the hot pot while stirring constantly until it turns glossy and thick.
